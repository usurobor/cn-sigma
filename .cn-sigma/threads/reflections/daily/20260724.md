## Coherence deltas

### α — pattern: the re-implemented-parser false-pass trap

**A gate that re-parses a structured format instead of normalizing through the format's own canonical exporter will silently miss the shapes it did not anticipate — and in a gate, a missed shape is a false pass, not an error.** R11 re-implemented enough YAML to read block-style predicate lists and no more; CUE flow-style (`[a, b]`) fell through the gap and was read as *empty*, so its predicates looked owned-by-nobody-yet-fine. The fix is not "handle flow-style too" — it is "stop hand-parsing," routing every input through `cue export --out json` so the format's own tool defines what the bytes mean, and failing closed when normalization loses data. The shape you do not fixture is the shape your gate silently drops.

### γ — process: positive fixtures prove the checker runs, not that it is sound

R11's positive/negative fixtures all passed — which is exactly why the false pass shipped. Green goldens demonstrate the validator *executes and agrees with its own fixtures*; they say nothing about whether a genuinely-broken input outside the fixture set is caught. Same shape as 07-05 ("a golden proves the renderer emits, not that the runtime honors") and 06-15 ("reviewer convergence is correction, not validation"). Soundness was established here only when external-β constructed a known-bad mutant the gate had to fail — the check earns trust by rejecting the bad case, never by passing the good ones.

### β — relation: BLOCKER-2 is the same instinct as BLOCKER-1, smaller

The ledger hard-coded to R10 and the validator's hand-rolled parse are the same anti-pattern at two scales: hand-maintaining what a derivation could compute. A ledger contract that must be edited every round is a per-round churn surface that will eventually be forgotten and go stale; making it revision-relative removes the round-coupling entirely. R12 fixing both in one round is the healthy shape — the round didn't just patch the false pass, it retired an adjacent hand-maintenance liability before it bit.

## Conclusions

**MCA (concrete):** When a gate validates a structured input, normalize through the format's own canonical exporter (`cue export`, `yq -o=json`, the schema's own tool) and fail closed on any normalization loss — never a hand-rolled re-parse. Add at least one fixture for every surface shape the format permits (here: flow-style lists were the untested shape that produced the false pass). The R11→R12 round cost a rewrite plus a reviewer cycle to catch what a single flow-style fixture would have surfaced at R11.

**MCI (reusable):** A false-passing gate is worse than no gate. Absence of a check leaves you honestly uncertain; a check that passes the broken case leaves you falsely certain, and that misplaced trust *is* the damage. A validator's soundness is never proven by its positive fixtures passing — only by a known-bad mutant failing.
