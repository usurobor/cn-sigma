## The lesson, front-loaded

Naming a rule is the easy half. A rule that ships without the mechanical check that enforces it is a wish, not a gate — it degrades silently exactly when it is most needed. And the discipline "make the check that binds" applies **reflexively**: to your own design captures, not only to the code under review. The strongest guard is a preregistered check-requirement — **no rule-issue is complete until it names its binding check.**

The proof this is load-bearing rather than tidy: over 07-27→28 the session filed five learning-capture issues, each proposing a rule against rules-without-checks — and the review caught that **every one of them proposed a rule and none proposed the check that makes it bind.** The session's own central gradient reproduced inside its own authorship. The discipline bit its author. That is the only kind of evidence that a rule is real.

## The meta-finding — the gradient reproduced in its own authorship

The whole point of the #671 wave, and of these captures, was that **a principle without a mechanism is inert** — the §8.6 warning the review cited: *"a rule only binds if someone applies it to themselves under deadline."* #676 encodes exactly that.

Then the reviewer's accepted meta-finding landed on the issues themselves: they wrote five rules-without-checks *about the danger of rules-without-checks.* The F1/F6/F7/F8 review notes are that same rule applied to the five bodies — amending each with the missing executable check (a negative fixture that MUST fail, a wired detector, a stated threshold, a two-frame seeding bar).

This self-application is more convincing than a clean capture would have been. A clean set would have proven the rules were *followed*. A set that caught its own authors omitting the discipline proves the discipline is *load-bearing* — it degrades under exactly the deadline pressure it names, and only a reflexive check catches it.

Note the pattern is **twice-observed**: 07-27's four issues, then 07-28's #678, both filed rule-first with the check supplied on review. Two observations within two days is enough to call it the working discipline, not a coincidence.

## Governing question to carry

*Does the rule you just wrote ship with the check that makes it bind — even when the "you" it must bind is the writing of the rule itself?*

## What to do differently

- **Rule and check are two acts; the second is the load-bearing one.** Naming the failure mode without shipping its mechanical gate is failure-laundering by another name.
- **Preregister the check-requirement.** No rule-issue is complete until it names its binding check: a negative fixture that MUST fail validation, a wired detector, a stated threshold. This is the reflexive guard — it fires on your own captures before a reviewer has to.
- **File a sharp review as typed debt against the ruleset it indicts, not as a thread.** Map the error log issue-by-issue (E3/E4/E5/E7 → #674, E2/E6 → #675) so no finding dissolves into "noted." Receipt discipline applied to review itself.
- **A finding without a receipt is the same bug one level up.** #676's whole content is that a requirement vanishing without a `FailureDisposition` is the failure — and #676 wiring to #677 makes the detector literal.
